# Learning by Design GenAI Assessment

Design or review an assessment where learner GenAI use is allowed, required,
limited, or prohibited. Read `references/assessment-patterns.md` before work.

Begin from the learning outcome, not from a tool or detection strategy. A final
product can remain valuable, but it is not automatically sufficient evidence
of learning in AI-mediated work.

## Design decisions

For each assessment, determine and state:

1. the knowledge or capability learners must develop;
2. whether AI use is prohibited, optional, required, or limited by phase, and
   the pedagogical reason;
3. what learners must do with AI outputs: compare, verify, critique, adapt,
   reject, redesign, or explain;
4. what proportionate evidence of process and judgment is needed;
5. how criteria assess the outcome rather than the polish of the output;
6. access, privacy, accessibility, language, and alternative-path provisions;
7. how feedback, revision, and collaboration contribute to learning.

## Deliverable

When designing, provide:

- assessment brief and learner-facing AI-use guidance;
- a process-evidence plan;
- analytic rubric criteria with observable performance descriptions;
- equity and implementation notes; and
- a source traceability note.

When reviewing, provide a gap table: `observed design`, `risk or missed
learning opportunity`, `recommended revision`, and `source basis`.

Avoid surveillance-oriented recommendations. Do not promise that authorship
can be established through AI detectors, prompt logs, or style comparison.
