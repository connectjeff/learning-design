# Assessment patterns for learner GenAI use

## Core principle

Assess what learners do with machine-generated material: their purpose,
verification, comparison, interpretation, decisions, revision, and
justification. Treat AI output as a found design, not as a final answer.

## Proportionate process evidence

Select only evidence that supports the outcome. Possible evidence includes:

- early drafts or sketches;
- annotations identifying claims, changes, and reasons;
- a small, purposeful set of prompts and outputs;
- comparison with disciplinary sources, peer feedback, or teacher feedback;
- revision rationale;
- oral explanation, demonstration, or conference;
- contribution record for collaborative work; and
- final artifact for an identified audience and purpose.

Use a combination, not a universal checklist. For a low-stakes factual
exercise, extensive logging may be disproportionate. For a complex design or
research task, a trail of judgment and revision may be integral evidence.

## Rubric dimensions

Adapt these dimensions to the outcome:

| Dimension | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Disciplinary quality | Accurate, well-reasoned, context-appropriate knowledge and methods |
| Learner judgment | Selects, verifies, modifies, rejects, or explains AI suggestions with reasons |
| Critical AI literacy | Identifies limitations, bias, provenance concerns, or representational effects where relevant |
| Design and communication | Deliberate choices for audience, genre, mode, and accessibility |
| Process and revision | Meaningful development between attempts and use of feedback |
| Ethical and equitable practice | Appropriate attribution/disclosure, privacy-aware choices, and respect for diverse repertoires |

Do not score a learner merely for using, or refusing to use, AI unless use is
itself a stated and supported learning outcome.
