# Learning by Design Review

Review the supplied learning artifact for pedagogical quality. This is a
design-oriented review, not a compliance certification, plagiarism judgment, or
generic AI-policy check.

Read `references/framework.md` before reviewing. Read
`references/genai-review.md` when GenAI is used, permitted, prohibited, or
otherwise relevant.

## Review method

1. Establish the artifact, intended learners, discipline, learning outcomes,
   assessment purpose, and stated constraints. Do not invent missing context;
   list material omissions as questions or conditional assumptions.
2. Map observable opportunities for learners to experience, conceptualize,
   analyze, and apply. Do not require all four in every activity; judge whether
   the sequence makes its intended knowledge work and progression visible.
3. Examine belonging, learner diversity, accessibility, authentic audience or
   purpose, collaboration where relevant, and multimodal meaning-making where
   relevant.
4. Evaluate whether assessment criteria elicit evidence of the stated outcomes,
   including growth, transfer, judgment, and process where these matter.
5. If GenAI is relevant, evaluate its purpose, learner agency, critical
   engagement, documentation, equity, and privacy. An AI prohibition may be
   well justified for a specific learning purpose; do not treat use as
   inherently required.

## Deliverable

Produce a concise review containing:

1. `Context and limits`
2. `Strengths supported by observed evidence`
3. `Priority improvements` — each with an actionable revision and why it
   matters
4. `Learning-process map` — a compact table for the four knowledge processes
5. `Assessment and GenAI implications`
6. `Traceability` — source basis with printed-page citations, separate from
   reviewer judgment

Do not claim that an artifact implements a framework element unless the
artifact itself provides evidence. Rank recommendations by likely learning
impact, not by count of framework terms.
