# Syllabus audit criteria

## Coherent learning design

Look for an intelligible route from outcomes through learning experiences to
assessment evidence. The route may recur or branch; it need not follow a
week-by-week four-stage pattern. A syllabus is stronger when it indicates how
learners will experience meaningful material, develop concepts, analyze
evidence and perspectives, and apply or redesign knowledge.

## Assessment

Look for explicit criteria and feedback opportunities that match the claimed
learning. Note when a grading scheme values only finished products while
outcomes include inquiry, collaboration, critical judgment, revision, or
responsible tool use.

## GenAI

An effective syllabus-level policy does not substitute for task design. For
each relevant task, clarify:

- the pedagogical purpose of any AI use or non-use;
- permitted boundaries and required disclosure;
- process evidence appropriate to the stakes;
- critical examination of output quality, bias, and limitations where relevant;
- access, privacy, disability, and language-repertoire considerations; and
- an equitable alternative where the tool is not available or appropriate.

Source basis: Kalantzis, Cope, and Zapata (2026, Ch. 5, pp. 109–29; Ch. 6,
pp. 143–66; Ch. 9, pp. 271–77, 283–84; Ch. 10, pp. 292–95). For full
traceability, see the repository’s `sources/learning-by-design-2026.md`.
